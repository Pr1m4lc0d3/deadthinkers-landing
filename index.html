The first three hundred are being chosen — balanced across centuries and traditions, so the shelves are not three hundred Western men. Their dates and birthplaces come from the public record rather than from a model, because a wrong birth date on a scholarly page costs everything else on it.

After that the library grows by being used. Summon a mind we don’t have yet, and the page you leave behind is waiting for whoever searches that name next. You would be writing the library.

He was not there

We asked Niccolò Machiavelli, dead since 1527, to assess the Cold War.

This is not a new thing. It is the old game of the great powers, played with new instruments. The Florentines and the Milanese, the Guelphs and the Ghibellines, the Pope and the Emperor — I have watched such rivalries consume Italy for centuries. The names change, the weapons change, but the pattern does not.

A state that must lie to its own people to survive is already dead. It merely has not yet fallen.

Niccolò Machiavelli — two passages from one unedited run of Deliberon’s persona engine, the engine Dead Thinkers is built on.

He knew perfectly well what the Cold War was. He can read the record as well as you can, and he opened by summarising it plainly — the dates, the proxy wars, the collapse. Then he did the thing worth having: he refused to treat it as new.

So the boundary is not what a mind knows. It is where it stands. He speaks from outside your century looking in — “in your century”, “the third world, as you call it” — and the frame he brings to 1962 is the one he built in Florence. That collision is the whole product. One run is not a guarantee, and we will publish more of them rather than ask you to take our word for it.

What has to happen
Pull the facts from the public record, write and read the prose before any of it is indexed, then wire the summoning to your own key.

We are not putting a date on it. There is more on the bench than can ship at once, and a date invented this morning would be the first thing on this site that wasn’t true. Leave an address and you get one letter, on the day it opens.
